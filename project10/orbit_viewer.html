Asteroid Orbit Viewer

A top-down view of the inner solar system. Each dot is an asteroid, moved along its real orbit. Press play, then turn on the gap rings to see where Jupiter has cleared out the belt.

Hover over, or tap, a dot to see its name and orbit.

What you are looking at

Positions are calculated from each asteroid's published orbit (size, stretch, tilt and where it was on a given date), using Kepler's equation. You look down on the plane of the solar system, with the Sun in the middle. Faint rings show where Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars and Jupiter orbit on average. With "Gap rings" on, the dashed rings sit at the orbit sizes of the Kirkwood gaps (2.06, 2.50, 2.82, 2.96 and 3.28 au).

What to try

Set the speed to 1 year / sec and watch the near-Earth objects (red) sweep in toward Earth's ring while main-belt asteroids circle in a steady band. Turn on the gap rings and check that thin stripes of the belt line up with them.

What this is not

Data: Minor Planet Center, MPCORB orbit file. Orbit sizes for the planets are approximate averages.
